about=Tembuku helps FTTH/GPON access-network designers plan feeder cables inside QGIS. Choose a folder with the existing cable data, the feeder design compilation, the new FDT points and the POP list (KMZ/KML, ZIP, GeoJSON, CSV/Excel, Shapefile or GeoPackage): Tembuku recognises each file by its content and reports missing or unreadable input clearly. For every new FDT it looks for a nearby feeder that still has a free tube and runs toward the FDT, picks the tap point, draws the new cable along existing poles and roads, checks the optical budget and recommends either reusing that feeder or building a new one from the POP, with the reason written out. Results are KMZ files in the compilation folder layout, styled QGIS layers, an offline HTML report and a CSV summary. You can also click an FDT on the map for an instant recommendation, choose another candidate in the results panel and add OpenStreetMap or satellite basemaps. Every setting can be changed. No extra Python packages needed; the internet is used only for OpenStreetMap road data (cached) and basemaps. Road data (c) OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. Reports are written in Indonesian. (14) COLO POLE FTTH and New Infra lines are now cut from the final drawn new cable, so they lie exactly on it (before: up to 8.7 m apart on Palembang H20), and hanger and 9 m pole points sit on the cable; a hanger next to the slack at the tap or the FDT is not written twice. (15) Finalisation rules of 17 Nov 2026: the new cable from the feeder to the FDT is 48 fibres when the 96F tube group it uses has 4 or fewer free tubes and 96 fibres when more are free (a 48F branch is always 48); an FDT north of the POP only uses a feeder whose tap point is north of the POP and an FDT south only one south (FDT or tap within 150 m of the POP's latitude are exempt); when every feeder is full a new feeder with the next number is proposed; the 'Dibuat oleh Tembuku ... OpenStreetMap' remark no longer shows in Google Earth (kept as a hidden KML comment, and the OpenStreetMap credit stays in the HTML report). 1.4.4 Engineering rules of 11 Nov 2026 checked line by line, against credible technical sources and against 213 approved designs in four cities; two rules are kept configurable but no longer applied literally by default, because the data and the sources show they cost money without improving the design. (1) Tube group by distance (6 km): with the rule applied literally, only 36 percent of F/T folders matched the designers and 563 km of new cable was proposed; with it off, 54 percent and 425 km (designers: 495 km). Optical loss depends on route length, not on fibre count (ITU-T G.652, G.984.2), so the reservation has no optical basis. A 48-fibre branch over 6 km is still rejected and a branch whose fibre path is over 6 km is a 96-fibre cable. (2) Taps at existing slack: preferred when the new cable is at most 600 m (or 15 percent) longer, instead of always. A closure can be built mid-span (manufacturers' mid-span access practice) and is marked 'please check'; reaching a slack with kilometres of extra cable is dearer than one closure. Settings measured: 300 m 421 km / 99 checks, 600 m 425 km / 92 checks and the highest F/T match, 1000 m 434 km / 81 checks, always 481 km / 60 checks. KMZ follows the APD template more closely: the exact slack or closure coordinate starts the new cable, Colo Pole lines take the colour of their cable size, closure 96-144 uses the Closure 96 icon, hanger and OLT icons follow the template, 9 m crossing poles are written in the Tiang 9 Meter folder, the New Infra folder is no longer added, every new cable carries a plain-language summary (decision, F/T, tube, new cable, share on existing poles, optical loss, poles), and the POP folder no longer ends in '.kmz'. A team icon folder (for example 'ICON APD FTTH') next to the data folder is found automatically. The template check now works at any folder depth. Fix: with a feeder tube record table (Excel/CSV) in the data folder, the tube of an FDT that is designed again (and of every design hidden in the accuracy test) stayed marked as used, so the next tube or even a new feeder was proposed; on the Palembang POP 3 data with its tube record the same F/T folder went from 2 to 31 of 60 and the same decision from 41 to 48 of 60. Faster: nearest-point and corridor checks no longer repeat work, with the same results. 1.4.3 Better and cheaper decisions, checked on 213 approved designs in four cities (Bengkulu, Gresik, Palembang, Serang), and results written in plain words. Tube choice: Tembuku now takes the lowest free tube of the feeder, as the designers do, instead of reserving T9-T12 for FDTs within 6 km; the 6 km limit for 48-fibre cables still applies and the old behaviour can be switched on under Advanced. Tap points: a tap at an existing slack, closure or cable end (end-to-end) is preferred when its new cable is at most 300 m (or 15 percent) longer, because a cable cannot be opened mid-span without spare cable; an FDT right next to a feeder is still joined near the FDT. A tap that is not at such a point, and every proposed tube number not yet recorded in the compilation, is marked 'please check', as the engineering rules ask. Among equal candidates the one with more of its new cable on existing poles is preferred. A closure proposed for one FDT can be shared by the next FDTs of the same run. Routes avoid footways, pedestrian streets and motorways (configurable). 9 m crossing poles are also placed where the route crosses residential and unclassified roads, and hangers are moved to the nearest proposed pole. Every result now starts with one plain sentence: what to build, which F/T folder, how much new cable, and what a new feeder would need instead; reasons for rejected candidates are written in everyday words. Results on the 213 designs, against 1.4.2: same decision 82 percent (unchanged), same F/T folder 37 to 53 percent, route match median 42 to 73 percent, and total proposed new cable 523 to 420 km (20 percent less). 1.4.2Four data-reading fixes found by running four city compilations (213 designs) on 12 Nov 2026; the design rules and the route limit of 1.24 are unchanged.
